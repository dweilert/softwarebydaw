What it does

A working list, not a wish list — every item below is real and shipping today.

See the actual calculation

The Engine Viewer shows the real intermediate values behind every number — RMD divisors, tax-bracket math, IRMAA tier assignment. Not a black box.

Social Security optimizer

Scores every claim-age combination for a household — a 9×9 grid including the spousal top-up — and ranks the best lifetime strategy. Ask for the finer answer and it searches every whole month from 62 to 70 for both of you, then re-runs the whole projection on the strongest candidates.

It tells you what its own shortcut costs

Every planner optimizes by testing a shortlist — checking every possibility is too slow. None of them tell you what the shortlist misses. This one does: ask it to check every Social Security claim age and it runs your full projection on all 9,409 combinations, paints the results as they land, then names the pair its fast answer picked, the best pair that exists, and the gap between them in dollars.

Roth conversion planner

Eight ranked conversion strategies with tax-bracket and IRMAA impact previewed before you commit to a ladder.

Tax-aware Monte Carlo

Every simulated path re-runs the full projection — taxes, RMDs, IRMAA — instead of just shocking a balance and freezing everything else.

Full tax & IRMAA modeling

Federal, state, and county/city income tax, capital gains, and Medicare IRMAA surcharge projection with the real two-year lookback.

Estate & legacy projection

Trusts, lifetime gifting, heir bequest splits, and inherited-account rules (10-year rule, EDB stretch) — plus an At-Death Checklist built from your own plan.

Historical backtest since 1928

Replays your plan through every real historical retirement start-year, cross-validated against two independent datasets.

Built-in guided training

43 interactive lessons walk you through the app screen by screen — not a video, not a PDF.

Local-first & private

No accounts, no cloud sync, no telemetry. Your plan never leaves your machine unless you export it yourself.

Said plainly: what you're trading away

No product is best at everything, and pretending otherwise wouldn't be honest:

  • No bank/brokerage account syncing — this is a private, offline tool by design.
  • No mobile app — desktop only, for macOS, Windows, and Linux.
  • No human coaching or advisor access, unlike Boldin's paid tier.
  • MaxiFi's Social Security and consumption-smoothing engines carry decades more academic refinement on similar underlying ideas.
  • ProjectionLab's freeform what-if exploration is still a smoother, more polished experience for quick, unstructured scenarios.

The macOS and Windows downloads are signed, and the build refuses to publish an installer whose signature does not verify — so a release either carries a valid one or does not exist. Linux packages are unsigned, as Linux packages normally are.

macOS: drag it to Applications

Signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple, with the notarization ticket stapled to the .dmg itself. There is no right-click, no "open anyway", and no Terminal command to run. Because the ticket travels inside the file, this works with no network connection too.

  1. Open the downloaded .dmg file.
  2. Drag the Retirement Planner icon onto the Applications folder shown in that same window — clicking or double-clicking the icon directly from the .dmg window will not work.
  3. Open it from Applications the normal way.
Windows: run the installer

Signed with a Microsoft-issued certificate, timestamped so the signature stays valid after the certificate itself expires. Double-click the installer and follow it through.

Windows may still show a SmartScreen warning for a while. That is not a problem with the signature — SmartScreen weighs how many people have installed builds carrying this certificate, and a new certificate starts with none. It goes away as downloads accumulate. If you meet it, check the publisher shown reads Dave Weilert.

Linux: mark the AppImage executable

AppImages aren't executable by default. Mark it once from a terminal, then run it directly (double-click, or ./ the file):

chmod +x ./Retirement.Planner_*.AppImage
