RP Methodology

RP is a planning model built to help a household or advisor compare decisions over time. The engine and math are shared by the Agency and Consumer editions; their workflows and user interfaces are different.

How a plan is evaluated

After-tax first

Cash-flow comparisons account for the tax consequences that change what a household can actually spend. Gross income alone is not the same as usable income, especially when withdrawals, Roth conversions, RMDs, Medicare premiums, or survivor filing status change.

Year-by-year projection

The engine carries income, spending, taxes, benefits, account balances, insurance and major events through the plan horizon. Each year's result becomes the starting point for the next year's calculation.

What-If comparisons

Strategies and scenarios change defined inputs and rerun the plan so the comparison is tied to the same household assumptions. The result is evidence for a conversation, not an instruction to buy or sell a product.

Risk and uncertainty

Historical and stochastic tools explore how a plan responds to different market, inflation, longevity, health-care and spending paths. Those tools describe uncertainty; they do not predict a single future.

Transparency and limits

Reference figures are cited and versioned. Validation cases are published separately. The model discloses areas it does not model, simplifies, or treats as an assumption. A plan should therefore be reviewed with the household's facts, goals, legal and tax circumstances, and professional advice where appropriate.
