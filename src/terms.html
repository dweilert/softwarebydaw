These terms cover RP-Hub, the service that sends e-mail and manages calendar events on behalf of an advisory practice. If your practice has a signed agreement with us, that agreement governs and these terms fill the gaps.

What RP-Hub does

Advisors work in RP, RP Practice CRM and RP DocMgmt. RP-Hub is what those products call when they need to reach outside: to send a message an advisor has composed, or to put a meeting the advisor has already scheduled onto their calendar.

It is not an inbox and does not replace Outlook or Gmail. It sends; it does not read.

Connecting a mailbox

By connecting a Google or Microsoft account you confirm that the mailbox is yours to use and that you may authorise software to send from it. What RP-Hub may do is exactly what that provider's consent screen listed — no more — and you can withdraw it at any time, from inside the product or directly with the provider.

Acceptable use

RP-Hub is for a practice's ordinary correspondence with its clients and counterparties. Do not use it to send unsolicited bulk e-mail, to send on behalf of someone who has not authorised it, or in any way that breaches your provider's terms or the law. A connection being used that way may be suspended.

Your content

What you send stays yours. We claim no ownership of it and handle it as described in the Privacy Policy.

Availability, and what we do not promise

RP-Hub depends on Google, Microsoft and other providers. We cannot guarantee that any particular message is delivered, and an outage or policy change at a provider can interrupt the service.

What we do instead is tell you the truth about each attempt. RP-Hub records the outcome of every send and reports it back, so a practice can see whether a message actually left, rather than assuming it did because a screen said so.

RP-Hub carries messages; it does not check them

RP-Hub does not review, approve or advise on what you send. Whether a message is accurate, suitable, and compliant with the rules governing your practice remains your responsibility.
